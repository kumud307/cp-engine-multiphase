Opening0:00 – 0:35
Say

“Our topic is the specific heat of multiphase fluids. The usual shortcut is to average the heat capacities of the components. That fails exactly where it matters: when liquids mix non-ideally, and when a phase is melting or boiling. So our engine never averages. It builds the enthalpy of the whole system as a state function and differentiates it: Cp = (∂H/∂T)P. Everything from the course enters through H. You can give it a composition and a temperature, or a calorimeter measurement, or an entropy change, and it finds every route to Cp that your inputs allow.”

Demo 21:15 – 2:00

Ethanol + water: the ideal-solution rule is 18 % low

Say

“30 mol % ethanol in water. The ideal-solution rule, Σ xiCp,i, gives 86.5 J/(mol K). The real value is 104.8. The difference is the excess heat capacity, which we get from the NRTL excess Gibbs energy with the Gibbs–Helmholtz equation: HE = −RT² ∂(GE/RT)/∂T, and CpE is its temperature derivative. The engine also reports partial molar heat capacities, and Euler's theorem holds: 0.3 × 138.85 + 0.7 × 90.22 gives back 104.81.”

Point atideal 86.47 → real 104.81 J/(mol K); CpE = 18.33; partial molar 138.85 (ethanol), 90.22 (water)

Demo 42:20 – 3:05

Ice slurry: the multiphase case

Say

“This is the core of the topic. 10 wt % ethanol in water at −5 °C is a slurry of ice in solution. Its apparent Cp is 52,800 J/(kg K), thirteen times liquid water, because ice melts as the temperature rises. The breakdown shows it: 3,600 ideal, 270 excess, 48,900 latent. The latent term is the chain rule: ∂H/∂n times dn/dT, with the ice amount from solid–liquid equilibrium, equal chemical potentials. For an energy balance you use the mean over the interval: 14,100 from −15 to +5 °C. And the integral of our Cp equals ΔH to seven digits, so no latent heat is lost or counted twice.”

Point at52,783.6 J/(kg K) = 3,616 ideal + 270 excess + 48,898 latent; interval mean 14,086.2

A mixture that boils

Say

“Ethanol boils at 78 °C, but 5 % ethanol in water at 85 °C is still liquid, because a mixture boils at its bubble point, here 90.3 °C. If the professor interruptsShort answers. Longer derivations are in the Concept Guide and the handbook.

Why differentiate H instead of averaging heat capacities?

An average misses the excess enthalpy and the change of phase amounts with temperature. dH/dT contains both and guarantees ∫CpdT = ΔH. The ice slurry is the proof: 52.8 against about 3.9 kJ/(kg K) for the average.

Where does the latent heat enter?

Chain rule: dH/dT = Σ n Cp (ideal) + n CpE (excess) + Σ (∂H/∂n)(dn/dT) (latent). Only the last term carries it, so it is counted once.

Why NRTL and not Margules as in the lecture?

Same framework (GE → γ → HE → CpE). NRTL handles more than two components and has temperature-dependent parameters, which CpE needs because it is a second temperature derivative of GE.

Where did the NRTL parameters come from?

We fitted them to measured excess enthalpies. Parameters from vapour–liquid data give CpE six or more times too small, because they fix GE but not its curvature in T. Boiling uses a separate set fitted to bubble points; one joint set doubled the Cp error, so we kept two and say so.

Why Peng–Robinson only for the vapour?

Cubic equations give liquid volumes 5–20 % off and worse caloric properties. Liquids use measured Cp correlations; the cubic is used where it is good.

Why γ–φ and not one equation of state for both phases?

Water + alcohols are strongly polar and associating; no constant kij fits both phases. The activity model fits the liquid to 0.26 K in bubble point.

The Cp at the melting point is huge. Is that physical?

A pure substance has an infinite Cp at Tm. We smooth the step over a small width, so the point value depends on that width and the engine warns. The latent heat and the interval mean do not depend on it; use those.

What can it not do?

Non-equilibrium states (supercooling, hysteresis), reacting systems, electrolytes beyond NaCl, and the near-critical region, where it now prints a warning. Emulsions and PCM slurries are not validated against experiments.

Can you run my example?

Yes: python -m cp_engine, type the components and T. Components available: water, ethanol, methanol, glycerol, acetone, n-hexane, toluene, n-dodecane, n-octadecane, Al2O3, CuO, SiO2, NaCl. For anything else, say “that needs a data entry in data/components.json; no code changes”.
